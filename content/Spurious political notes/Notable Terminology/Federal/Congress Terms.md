
# Bills and Vote Related Terms
### **Voting up or down rule**
- direct vote in house or senate on an amendment bill
- used by people frustrated by opponents who delay a bill indefinitely with various tactics
	- rules of house/senate allow a bill to be delayed, so you are using the "rule" to obstruct
- voting up means to vote yes to pass a bill and move it forward
- voting down mean to vote no and reject the bill
- **Unamendable:** This method is typically used when a bill is brought to the floor under strict rules that prohibit lawmakers from changing its text, allowing them only to vote for or against the core substance
- **Procedural Bypass:** It intentionally bypasses stalling tactics (such as a motion to table or a filibuster) to force a definitive decision on the proposal itself
- **Majority Rules:** If the "up" votes meet the required threshold—usually a simple majority (50% plus 1) in the House or a three-fifths margin (60%) to break a filibuster in the Senate—the bill passes. If it falls short, it is voted down.

### **Tabling**
- a vote to put a motion under consideration and can't be voted on until you vote to bring it up again
	- usually used to discard the motion (like to discard voting on a bill)
- Not federal specific, applies to any parliamentary procedure

### **Recommit**
- a motion to recommit means send piece of legislation back to committee that previously reviewed it
	- essentially bounces the bill
- Use to make specific amendments of a bill to to effectively kill it, since they would need to vote to commit the bill again, which would be shot down

### **Second degree amendments**
- amendment of an amendment
- tertiary amendments are not allowed, so this is the final stage a bill can take place
