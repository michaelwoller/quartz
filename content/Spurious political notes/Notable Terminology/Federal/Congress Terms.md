
# Bills and Vote Related Terms

### **Filibuster**
- Senate tactic where senators delay or block a vote on a bill by refusing to stop debating it
	- can't vote on bill while debate is still open, so if you keep talking, bill never moves
	- Could go on for over 24 hours
		- Strom Thurmond spoke for over 24 hours against the Civil Rights Act of 1957
- Not in the constiution
- For the most part, nobody has to talk anymore. There's an **implied filibuster** 
	- 1975 senate created a **dual track** system, which means you can set a filibustered bill aside and work on other business while filibuster is technically still happening
		- so implied filibuster a bill, while doing other votes
			- this lets you move to **cloture**
	- if you invoke this, it triggers a 60 vote (three fifths supermajority) threshold requirement
		- This is due to **cloture**, which is a procedural vote that ends debate, needing 60 votes
			- if it passes, then bill proceeds to a normal majority vote
			- usually requires bipartisan cooperation since neither party usually has 60 seats
			- however, once cloture is invoked (60 votes obtained), the rules allow 30 more hours of debate before the final vote.
				- senators sometimes use that tme to make a case on the record or just be difficult
		- this means in practice, almost any bill needs 60 votes to move forward in the senate, not a simple majority
- Defenders say it protects the minority party and forced compromise
- critics say it gives the minority party a de facto veto over the majority's agenda and has been used to be obstructionist
- Sometimes senators still do long speaches "talking filibusters"
	- but these are largely theatrical to generate attention

### **Voting up or down rule**
- direct vote in house or senate on an amendment bill
- used by people frustrated by opponents who delay a bill indefinitely with various tactics
	- rules of house/senate allow a bill to be delayed, so you are using the "rule" to obstruct
- voting up means to vote yes to pass a bill and move it forward
- voting down mean to vote no and reject the bill
- **Unamendable:** This method is typically used when a bill is brought to the floor under strict rules that prohibit lawmakers from changing its text, allowing them only to vote for or against the core substance
- **Procedural Bypass:** It intentionally bypasses stalling tactics (such as a motion to table or a filibuster) to force a definitive decision on the proposal itself
- **Majority Rules:** If the "up" votes meet the required threshold—usually a simple majority (50% plus 1) in the House or a three-fifths margin (60%) to break a filibuster in the Senate—the bill passes. If it falls short, it is voted down.

### **Tabling**
- a vote to put a motion under consideration and can't be voted on until you vote to bring it up again
	- usually used to discard the motion (like to discard voting on a bill)
- Not federal specific, applies to any parliamentary procedure

### **Recommit**
- a motion to recommit means send piece of legislation back to committee that previously reviewed it
	- essentially bounces the bill
- Use to make specific amendments of a bill to to effectively kill it, since they would need to vote to commit the bill again, which would be shot down

### **Second degree amendments**
- amendment of an amendment
- tertiary amendments are not allowed, so this is the final stage a bill can take place

### **Reconciliation Bill**
- The Congressional Budget Act of 1974 create reconciliation process to get budget-related legislation passed more efficiently
- essentially a budget bill that is a fast-track tool to bypass the 60-vote Senate filibuster.
	- It allows bills changing federal spending, revenues, and debt limit to pass with simple majority
- **The Budget Resolution:** Congress must first pass a concurrent budget resolution that includes "reconciliation instructions" directing specific committees to draft legislation meeting certain financial targets.
- **Committee Action:** The designated committees draft and approve their pieces of the legislation. 
- **Bundling:** The Budget Committee combines these pieces into a single comprehensive package
- **Fast-Track Debate:** In the Senate, debate time is limited to 20 hours, preventing the minority party from mounting a filibuster
- **The Byrd Rule:** Provisions within the bill must be strictly related to the budget. The "Byrd Rule" prevents policy changes that have no significant fiscal impact or that increase the deficit beyond a 10-year window. 
	- helps reconsiliation not be abused by partisans
- Reconciliation can be abused to defacto create new agencies just by providing funds to current agencies to do more things
	- aka, to create new agencies officially, they'd need 60 votes to get past the filibuster
	- Instead, just fast-track reconciliation, give a department more money. the department then defacto does things that it didn't do originally
