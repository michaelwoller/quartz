# Preface

This marks when Lovecraft started getting published in pulp magazines. We also see him starting to develop some of his mythos with "The Tomb" and "Dagon". 

1908: [The Alchemist](https://www.hplovecraft.com/writings/fiction/a.aspx)
- Synopsis:
	-  Antoine, the last of an ancient French noble line, grows up alone in a crumbling ancestral château, haunted by a family curse: no Comte of his line has ever survived past the age of thirty-two. A family document reveals the origin of the curse. A medieval peasant alchemist named Michel Mauvais was unjustly killed by an ancestor during a panicked search for his missing son Godfrey, who turned up safe only after Michel was already dead. Michel's son Charles Le Sorcier swore revenge and threw a fatal potion in the Comte's face before vanishing into the night. As Antoine approaches thirty-two, he discovers a secret underground chamber in the castle and confronts a gaunt and ancient figure who reveals he has personally carried out the murders across six centuries, kept alive by the Elixir of Eternal Life he perfected. The figure is Charles Le Sorcier himself.

1917, June: [The Tomb](https://www.hplovecraft.com/writings/fiction/t.aspx)
   - Synopsis:
	-  Jervas Dudley is a dreamy, solitary young man who as a child stumbles upon the sealed granite tomb of the Hyde family hidden in a wooded hollow near his home. He becomes obsessed with entering it, spending years trying to force the padlocked door. One night he wakes outside the tomb to hear strange voices within, and afterward discovers a key in his attic that opens the vault with ease. From that night forward he haunts the tomb, and undergoes a profound transformation: his speech becomes archaic, he develops a bold and reckless manner entirely unlike his former character, and he begins reciting 18th-century drinking songs he has never read. He also develops a terror of fire and thunderstorms. Eventually he is discovered and restrained one night during a storm, babbling about his right to be buried in the Hyde coffin that bears his name. His father insists the padlock was never touched and that Jervas only ever slept outside the tomb in a trance. The story closes with the one piece of evidence that cannot be explained away: his loyal servant breaks open the tomb and finds, on a slab in an alcove, an empty coffin whose tarnished plate reads simply "Jervas."

1917, July: [Dagon](https://www.hplovecraft.com/writings/fiction/d.aspx)
- Synopsis:
	-  A morphine-addicted narrator writes a final confession before throwing himself from a window. He recounts how he escaped a German naval vessel during World War I and drifted adrift until he woke on a vast plain of black mud, apparently a section of ocean floor thrown to the surface by a volcanic upheaval. Trekking across the wasteland, he descends into a canyon and discovers a colossal ancient monolith carved with aquatic hieroglyphics and bas-reliefs depicting humanoid fish-creatures worshipping undersea idols. Then a titanic scaled being rises from the dark water and embraces the monolith in worship. The narrator goes mad, and after being rescued at sea can never escape the memory or the morphine he used to suppress it. He ends his account hearing something massive pressing against his door before leaping from the window. The story is an early and important entry in what would become the Cthulhu mythology.

1917: [A Reminiscence of Dr. Samuel Johnson](https://www.hplovecraft.com/writings/fiction/rdsj.aspx)
- Synopsis:
	-  The narrator reveals he has been passing as a young man born in 1890 but confesses he is actually 228 years old, born in Devonshire in 1690, and was personally acquainted with Dryden, Pope, Addison, and Swift. The bulk of the piece consists of fond but gently deflating anecdotes about Dr. Samuel Johnson and the Literary Club: Johnson's slovenly appearance and devastating wit, Boswell's teasing and alcoholic mannerisms, Goldsmith's vanity, and Gibbon's odious sneering manner. The joke, sustained throughout in pitch-perfect 18th-century prose, is that the immortal narrator is thoroughly outclassed by every famous man around him and repeatedly bested in conversation by Johnson. It is a rare purely comic piece from Lovecraft with no horror whatsoever, showcasing his deep affection for Augustan English prose style.
