Things I've seen Daoism compared with:

*Bodhisattva* -- Enlightened (Bodhi) being (sattva) in Buddhism. Anyone on a path towards Buddhahood (separate religion than Taoism though)
- preaches being there while getting there
- oneness is inseparable with noneness

*Menschkeit* -- Yiddish/German term to describe essence of good human being. Embodies moral uprightness and communal responsibilities.
- The space between a handshake: a cultural idea that true relationships and trust are built on honor and mutual respect. 

*Presencing* -- blending presence (being in the moment) with sensing (feeling of future possibilities)
- A teaching method in Dharma Buddhism

I guess I am skeptical of over emphasizing the comparisons here, since there are centuries of bickering between the religions. 
- Both buddhism and Taoism speek on "emptiness", but for over thousands of years Taosists and Buddhists have been under the impressions that what they speak on are different, not there can't be similarities per say. 
	- "emptiness" is very time and cultural specific. Ancient Greeks might have spoken on similar notions on the surface, but could have meant different things when you actually think on them, for example

More o that end, writings of Plotinus, Advaita Vedanta, Lao-Tzu, Buddhist writings, and such all have similar notions of "emptiness" that might seem similar in that they are not empty and do not abscribe to duality. But they differ from each other. Taoism and Buddhist voids are not the same. Similar concepts on surface, but have real distinctions
- keep this in mind to not have surface level understanding
- But even then, Neo-Platonists had hundreds of commentaries and physical exercises/rituals that are lost
	- These traditions are thousands of years old, really not precise to boil them down and how they developed to mix with eachother
		- Have differences in dualism vs non-dualism, monists, the supreme good, the void, etc.
			- [e.g.](https://www.reddit.com/r/taoism/comments/mug25g/comment/gv7rz8f/?utm_source=share&utm_medium=web3x&utm_name=web3xcss&utm_term=1&utm_content=share_button)  between buddhists and hindus "*Sanskrit citta* refers to awareness, consciousness, while Greek νους *nous* refers more to intellect, something Ventanists contrast *citta* against. *Citta* refers more to our consciousness, in which our intellect operates, while *nous* refers to the intellectual operations themselves. This distinction becomes incredibly important in debates between Hindus and Buddhists throughout the Middle Ages, but the Greeks never really talked about it. So you cannot say they are the same. In this sense Buddhist and Vedantists overlap a bit, but they disagree with their Greek contemporaries."
				- "It's easy to confuse these terms if you rely on English glosses, and to then start to see a general 'sameness' when in fact these traditions disagreed with each other. "
				- "The Dao isn't 'the Good News'. It's a practice to be cultivated and one requires correction to practice. Nobody's practice improves without correction"

Tao Te Ching is next to the Bible with being one of the most translated texts of all time, yet it does not advocate for religious institutions.
- it is popular for over 2500 years due to its own merits
- it says some of the most with the least words. It's like 50 pages long
- It changes as people change. It is never the same (granted this can be said for a lot of deep books)
	- because it is like a reflection of oneself

Lao Tzu does not ask to accept anything on blind faith for he is not a prophet or messiah. He asks you to trust the lessons in the Tao Te Ching will prove themselves
- The lessons are practical as a consequence. It is a guide of how to live life
