# The social relations model

There is a hidden but important assumption in the matching phenomneon, observers must agreee who has the traits and who doesn't. 

Organisms who evolved to assess one another's traits for future reproductive success need to demonstrate reasonable amount of consensus on who has those traits.

Matching studies often work on very obvious traits that are observable.

We need a *Social Relations Model (SRM)*. This tool examines how people perceive and understand others (page 33) (this is used for more than romance)
- any subjective judgement one person makes about another ("he is sexy" ," i want to date her" etc) is the sum of three factors
	- consensus factor (the person the judgement is about)
	- selectivity factor (person making the judgement)
	- compatibility factor (unique combination of the two people)

SRM might be the best tool social science can use for human social relationships.
**ONLY THE FIRST ONE** catches mate value.
E.g.
- consensus: she is a knock out
- selectivity: I am desperate enough for any attentions 
- compatibility factor:  unique aspects that are only appealing to that individual
	- above their popularity and your desperation
		- she is uniquely amazing in a way no others are

I might judge someone on sexiness, but you might judge her on her obnoxiousness. 
- mismatch due to comatability factor
	- e.g. I guess I am more attracted to neurotic women because I find them relatable and feel like they could relate to me more. It would be very difficult describing to someone who hasn't really felt with many emotional troubles all the things I experience. Not that it isn't possible, it would jsut make them lower on my compatibility factor

the three factors are not mutually exclusive.  nor are they equally important. Issue is different people can value different things and depending on the person, attractiveness is valued higher (pg 36 for more)

You can't really think your way out of these three factors. They occur unconsciously. 
You can't really reason out if you think a person's attractiveness is due to consensus or compatability. To do so you would need access to many people's minds
- an individual cannot do this but a psychologist can with data

Evoscript says consensus should play the largest, if only role. But thisisn't what we see, but if consensus value is low, it becomes much less predictive (in the middle to low ends)

ITS ABOUT MOST PREDICTIVE MODEL. Evoscript can be predictive, sometimes well predictive, but is it always the most predictive?

some contexts consensus will be more predictive, other times it will not be, but there's margins of error

	- the more people you ask the more variance there will be 

Evoscript might say ya but you can't ignore the effect, but that's true, the issue is that incoporrating other aspects and not just valuing attractiveness and mate valuye as all leads to a more predictive model
