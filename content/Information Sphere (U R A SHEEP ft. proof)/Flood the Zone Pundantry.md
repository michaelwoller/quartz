The flood the zone disinformation tactic is something I feel no one understands properly. This tactic refers to how people will just spit out lies and half-truths at such speed and with such impunity that an individual simply can't sort them out. It's not that they are stupid, but to figure out who is right or wrong would take a full-time job, so you just have to go with people you trust. This is supposed to be the job of pundits, who are people whose political beliefs and instincts you trust to break down information and regurgitate it to you in an easy and consumable way.

The issue is that if someone posits themselves as a pundit with a simple, appealing worldview, they can easily control the confusion arising from the cacophony. 

Aka, thinking "I can't make heads or tails of this stuff" leads to either 
a) checking out of politics
OR
b) putting all faith in punditry

Pundits know this, so they are incentivized to keep creating and pushing this environment since it benefits them. The people who control the pundits (such as the Republican overheads) can use this to their benefit. Other extremists stand to gain from it by making other pundits that are too hard to understand or are not world affirming less trustworthy. 

I have seen this with my conservative family. I have seen this with my leftist friends. They just don't trust anything anymore, but their populist demagogues on the right/left appeal to their sensibilities.

This was always true to some extent, but it's gotten so much worse and is completely out of control. I genuinely don't like self-serving thinking (i.e., I'm so much more rational and smarter than everyone else to fall for bullshit), but it drives me insane that hardly anyone understands the information sphere. I incredibly sympathize with Destiny's frustrations of being one of the only people online who get it.

Idk how this is solved without a heavy crackdown and redefining what it means to have "free speech," especially online. I don't think it's a good idea to censor or start arresting people over their words online, but *something* has to be done. There's still so much pushback on these ideas, even from some people who agree that there's an issue.

There's this idea I've heard that politicians or people running for politics need to swear in that they will tell the truth whenever they go on a public program. A lot of people really disliked that solution. But then all that's going to happen is people will continue to lie and go on platforms that will let them lie, and the people who care about the truth and honesty will get punished for it, since they will be attacked and when they admit their mistakes and change their mind, their credibility will be hurt. Compared to the liars and/or populists who can retain the same position and walk out of it seeming more credible and dedicated.

From my experience talking to people in real life about it, they get defensive about what information they consume or deny it. But this isn't even a partisan lib vs leftist/maga issue. It's a very bad societal issue that we _all_ need to worry about.
