
Random thoughts:

"Flood the zone" disinformation is misunderstood. The goal isn't to convince anyone of specific lies, it's to overwhelm. When lies and half-truths come fast enough, sorting them out becomes a full-time job nobody has. So people either check out of politics entirely or outsource their judgment to pundits they trust.

Pundits are incentivized to maintain this environment. Confusion is their business model. And the people above them, party leadership, media owners, ideological operators, benefit too because a confused public is a dependent public. Fringe actors pile on because chaos delegitimizes everyone harder to understand than a populist with a simple story.

I've watched this happen to my conservative family and my leftist friends. They've both arrived at the same place: total distrust of institutions, total faith in their respective demagogues. The mechanism is identical even if the flavor differs.

I resist the self-serving conclusion that I've simply seen through it while everyone else hasn't. But the information environment is genuinely broken in a way most people don't have the framework to diagnose. And there's no clean solution. Censorship creates its own problems. Oath requirements for politicians get gamed immediately, since liars will just find platforms with no accountability while the honest ones get punished every time they admit uncertainty or change their mind.

The deeper problem is that nobody wants to hear this is about them too. People get defensive about their media diet, or they frame it as a partisan issue. It isn't. It's structural, and it's getting worse.
