# City Leaders

All of these are elected 
## Mayor
- The mayor is the chief executive officer and is elected every four years. 
- There is no city manager, the mayor holds executive powers
- Powers
	- Appoints general managers and comisioners
	- Can remove officials from city posts
	- The  Mayor is also required to propose a budget each year
	- Has veto and emergency powers
	- Has influence over annual budget and enforcement of city ordinances

## City Council
- Los Angeles City Council is lawmaking body
- 15 members for 15 districts 
- Each district contains approximately 260,000 residents and is represented by an elected council member
- powers 
	- Votes on proposed laws and budgets for city
	- informal power over development, transportation, and other city services within their own district
	- council approves mayor's appointments and proposals

## City Attorney
- An elected official who serves as the city's chief legal counsel. 
- Prosecutes misdemeanor crimes and represents the city in civil legal matters

## City Controller
- chief accounting officer
- auditor and paymaster of LA
	- oversees audit services, accounting operations, and financial reporting
- Analyze the effectiveness of different city departments
- keeps data on how spend money
	- holds mayor office accountable

## City Clerk
- maintains official city records and manages elections
- oversees divisions covering council and public services , record management, elections, and land records

# Key Staff/Roles

Appointed by Mayor and/or city council
### Chief Administration Officer (CAO)
- helps the mayor prepare annual city budgets and helps the council analyze the city budget. 
- appointed jointly by mayor and cty council (considered a shared resource between the two) and reports to both (the only appointed officer to do so)

### Chief Legislative Analyst (CLA)
- Helps city council develop legislative programs, analyze budgets on behalf of council, procedure research on legislative matters
- Appointed and answers to the city council

### City Treasurer
- manages city's funds and investments
	- manages general and special pool investment portfolio and debt programs exceeding %50 billion
- appointed by Mayor with council confirmation

# Major City Departments

### Public Safety
- Los Angeles Police Department (LAPD)
	- Law enforcement
	- Governed by Board of Police Commissioners
	- Specialized units for gangs, narcotics, SWAT, and air support
- Los Angeles Fire Department (LAFD)
	- Fire suppression
	- emergency medical services
	- building review for fire saftey
	- governed by the Fire Commission
### Public Works and Infrastructure 
- Los Angeles Department of Transportation (LADOT)
	- Oversees parking, traffic control, DASH buses, bicycle infrastructure
	- governed by Board of Transportation Commissioners
-  Los Angeles Department of Water and Power (LADWP)
	- the largest municipal supplier of water and power utilities in the nation
	- funds are not tied to the city but come from ratepayers (aka, bills people pay on utilities)
		- still have to create budgets: $6 billion+ budget
			- seperate funds: Power Revenue Fund and Water Revenue Fund
- Department of Building and Safety (LADBS)
	- Provides permitting, plan check, inspection, and code enforcement for residential and commercial buildings
	- governed by the Board of Building and Safety Commissioners
- Los Angeles Department of Public Works (LADPW)
	- Oversees streets, sidewalks, street trees, lighting, sanitation, trash collection, recycling, sewage
	- Has several bureaus
		- Bureau of Sanitation
		- Bureau of Street Lighting
		- Bureau of Street Services
		- Bureau of Engineering
		- Bureau of Contract Administration

### Airports and Ports
- Los Angeles World Airports (LAWA)
	- owns and operates LAX and Van Nuys Airport (VNY)
	- Governed by seven-member Board of Airport Commissioners
	- funds are self generated from revenues from airpoat activities:
		- landing fees, terminal rents, concessions
			- 65% is a terminal rent paid by airlines and landing fees (aeronautical)
			- 35% are parking, concessions, rental cars, transportations, etc. (non-aeronautical)
		- not paid by taxpayer money
- Harbor Department (Port of Los Angeles)
	- located in San Pedro Bay
	- governed by the Los Angeles Board of Harbor Commissioners
		- not supported by city taxes, generates its own revenues from leasing ports and shipping service fees

### Housing and Planning
- Department of City Planning
	- Creates plans for housing production, approves new construction, and ensures businesses conform to zoning regulations
	- governed by nine commissions
		- City Planning Commission (CPC)
			- members serve without a salary
			- provide recommendations
		- Seven Area Planning Commissions (APCs)
			- largely serve as appeals boards for projects issued by City Planning or Zoning Administrators
			- Each major region has its own commission that oversee the areas
				- North Valley, South Valley, West LA, Central LA, East LA, South LA, Harbor
				- consist of five private citizens who do not get a salary
		- Cultural Heritage Commission (CHC)
			- Oversees the designation and protection of historic landmarks and cultural heritage sites
- Housing and Community Investment Department (HCID) or Los Angeles Housing Department (LAHD)
	- administers programs related to tenant protections and affordable housing production
	- three city commissions and four advisory groups
		- Rent Adjustment Commission
			- adopts policies, rules, and regulations to carry out mandate of Rent Stabilization ordinances
		- Affordable Housing Commission (AHC)
			- give strategic policy to improve and expand affordable housing in LA
		- Mobile Home Park Task Force (MHPTF)
			- specific forum for discussion of mobile home parks
- Housing Authority of the City of Los Angeles (HACLA)
	- Runs the city's stock of public housing
	- Governed by a seven-member board of commissioners responsible for setting policy, managing fiscal oversight, and appointing the authority's president and chief executive officer
		- Two commissioners must *live* in public housing
		- derives its authority from state law not just city charter, so it has some degree of independence. 
			- it is not beholden to the mayor's office
### The Department of Recreation and Parks

# Neighborhood Councils
- City council has a direct line of communication with 99 local neighborhood councils that serve as advisory groups
- civilian bodies that give communities a form voice in city decisions (these are non-binding)

# Los Angeles United School District (LAUSD)
- is enshrined in the LAS city charter, but operates independently of City hall
	- so mayor and city council have no direct control over the school board
- school board members are elected directly, not appointed
	- Seven member Board of Education who are elected from separate districts
	- the board appoints a superintendent who runs daily operations
- Has domain over K-12 schooling

# Los Angeles Community College District (LACCD)
- Community college district serving LA and neighboring cities of LA county
	- largest community college district in the US
- Has 9 colleges LA City College, East LA College, LA Harbor College, LA Mission College, LA Pierce College, LA Southwest College, LA Trade-Tech College, LA Valley College, and West LA College
- governed by The Board of Trustees for LACCD
- separated from the state level California Community Colleges Chancellor's Office, which sets statewide academic standards, but does not control individual district operations
- Residents in places like Santa Monica, Pasadena, Glendale, or Long Beach are served by entirely separate community college districts with their own elected boards

# LA Metro
- regional transit authority (buses, subway, light rail)
- Not a city or county department
- governed by board that includes LA mayor, county supervisors, and different city council representatives
- Spans multiple cities and counties, so nobody fully controls it

# Los Angeles Homeless Services Authority (LAHSA)
- Is co-operated between the city and county level operations
- Governed by commision
	- 5 individuals appointed by Mayor
	- 5 individuals appointed by Los Angeles County Board of Supervisors
- Also controlled by other advisory boards
	- board of people with lived experience of homelessness
	- a regional homeless advisory council
	- a continuum of care board
	- coordinate entry system council
- Still beholden to requests from city and county
- Generally has a very messy organization structure
	- so it is hard to understand who is incharge of what
	- kind of falling apart.
		- in April 2025, LA county board of supervisors moved to terminate contract with LAHSA due to issues found with audits and court-mandated financial review

# LA Superior Court
- ran by the CA state judicial branch, not county or city
- LA County District Attorney and LA City Attorney help prosecute crimes, but are beholden to the State's codes
	- they don't have governence over the courts
# Budget Cycle and Fund info

### Yearly cycle
- Sep-Nov
	- departments build their requests
		- salaries, wages, new/ongoing initiatives
		- send proposed annual budget to Mayor
- Nov-April
	- private negotiations between mayor and departments
		- meet with mayor and staff + CAO to review each department's proposed budget.
		- also develop revenue projections with Office of Finance, CAO, and Controller to know how much money the city will actually have before deciding how to spend it.
- April 20th
	- Mayor proposes a budget 
		- summarizes mayor's priorities, revenue, and appropriation exhibits
		- refered to as the "Blue Book"
			- details proposed changes for every department program
	- coincides with Mayor's state of the City address
- May
	- Council Budget and Finance Committee hearings
		- The city council's budget and finance committee reviews proposals and makes recommendations to the Full City Council
			- many day long hearings with Mayor's office, general managers, CAO, CLA, and other staff
				- can go any weeks
	- Five-member budget and finance comittee is the council's budget power and has enormous influence over what gets changed and what survives
	- CLA provides independent analysis from a legislature's perspective to give the Council a counterweight to the Mayor's numbers
- June 1
	- The charter requires the city council (majority vote) to adopt or modify the proposed budget BY June 1 (usually gets approved in May), but June 1 is a target
		- Mayor has five days to approve or veto
			- Council can override this with two-thirds vote
			- will go back and forth throughout June
				- Required to be done by July 1
					- If failed, there is no city shut down. California municipal law just has the city continue to operate under the prior year's adopted budget until the new fiscal budget is set
						- Has never actually happened, but came close in 2025
- July 1
	- new fiscal year
	- CAO's office monitors the city's finances
	- midyear adjustments are possible by council, but most be voted on and can be vetoes by Mayor

### General Fund and Special Funds
- The main discretionary budget is the General Fund
	- this is what departments fight over
	- Pension and awsuit obligations consume an increasibly large share of the general fund
		- LACERS and LAFPP
- Special funds are restricted for things like departments of water and power, port , airports 
	- these operate largely on their own revenues and budgets approved by their own commissions

### The Budget and Finance Committee
- hearings tend to focus operational spending (i.e, on staffing salaries, benefits, etc.)
	- visible, politically legible, directly tied to constituents (so it is heavily politicized)
		- so city council members often spend most time fighting for constituent staff funding
- little attention paid to capital expenditures (that identify consensus on needs or new priorities)
	- less political returns so less focus
	- infrastructure projects usually get the first cut
	- leads to lagging infrastructure investments
	- no capital planning process built in to the budget cycle like many other cities (which have separate multi-year capital improvement plans) that run *alongside* the budget with their own dedicated revenue streams, bond programs, and planning schedules
		- aka, if revenue becomes scarce, there is nothing guaranteed for infrastructure
	- Dedicated funding streams can occur in bond measures like Measure HHH for housing, school bonds, transportation measures, etc.
		- aka, set aside specific sources of funding
		- these are generally hard to coordinate and are siloed

### City Council budget authority and power
- Council holds final budget authority so individual council members use budget process as leverage over the mayor, departments, and other council members
	- Unlike other cities like NYC, Chicago, Houston) where the mayor has near-total executive authority over the budget
		- there, councils can amend and must approve of the budget, but the mayor's proposals dominate, so council's modifications are marginal at the edges (would need to look at city specifics for info)
	- corruption is a big issue here, like there was a scandal a few years ago
		- they allocated more funds to downtown
- The existence of the CLA gives the city council analytic edge to challenge the Mayor's proposals (something that doesn't exist in other cities)
